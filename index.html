This is a real OpenStreetMap basemap (via MapLibre GL JS + OpenFreeMap, no API key required), recolored to match the Scriptorium palette. Roads, coastline, lakes, and turloughs are real survey data, not illustrated. Major road names are shown; minor roads, tracks, and route-number shields are hidden to keep the map quiet. Nearby markers only merge into a numbered cluster once they'd actually overlap on screen, and that radius shrinks further as the discipline pills filter more points off the map, so a thinned-out map declutters faster — click a cluster to zoom in and split it apart. Towns never disappear into one of those anonymous clusters; if something else lands right on top of a town at low zoom, the town stays as its own marker with a small badge in the corner showing how many more are stacked there, and clicking that badge zooms in until they separate. A legend tied to a site or town sits just beside it on the map — close enough to read as the same place, but as its own separate marker in the Lore colour with the spiral icon — and each one's drawer carries a link chip straight to the other, so you can hop between the tale and the place it's about in one click. The "Routes" menu in the top bar suggests themed trails between sites — open a site's drawer and look for "Part of…" to jump to a trail it belongs to. Several coordinates are approximate pending field verification (dashed ring): Seven Streams of Teeskagh, Carran Turlough, the Carron and New Quay village centres, St Brigid's Well, and three legends with no site of their own yet on the map — Glasgeivnagh Hill/Slievenaglasha, Hag's Head, and Kilnaboy Church. Eyebright and "Burren Karst" describe a species and a landscape, not a single point, so they aren't pinned. The "SAT" button (top-left, next to the 3D toggle) swaps the base map for real aerial/satellite imagery — Esri World Imagery, no account needed, imagery © Esri, Maxar, Earthstar Geographics, and the GIS User Community — with roads, labels, and all markers still on top of it; click it again to go back to the recoloured map. Faint parchment contour lines are draped over the terrain throughout — real elevation data (AWS's open Terrarium tiles), not illustrated — and the ground itself gains real 3D relief once you tilt into the isometric view with the "3D" button.
